# The Drow Incident

> **Classification.** Open-source operational analysis. Subject: the precipitating event of the second half of the [[Fish People]] arc. Date of incident: **9/11/24** (Session 9). Location: bioluminescent-algae cavern beneath [[Fort Elm]], roughly halfway between the gulch entry and the [[The Ancient Ruined City|ancient ruined city]].
>
> This page documents the prevailing party-table assessment that the incident was not a chance encounter but a **targeted operational asset insertion** by the **[[Raiden Hand|Raiden Hand]]** (the operator known to convene game sessions).

## Stated facts of the incident

1. The party — [[Lyrion Thalor]], [[Eldrin Thalor]], [[Seren Moss]], [[Bell]], [[Thorn]], [[Teron]] — descended into a previously unseen cavern through Bell's gulch, masks in hand.
2. Combat began with Sahuagin, a Sahuagin Priestess, and a Sahuagin Champion.
3. Among the **bound, unconscious prisoners** rescued from the fish-monsters' attackers was **one (1) Drow commoner**, presented as a non-combatant captive without prior briefing to the party.
4. [[Lyrion Thalor|Lyrion]] killed the Drow prisoner with a spell, **without conscious deliberation**.
5. [[Teron]] (Paladin of [[Mystra]]) shield-bashed Lyrion in immediate response.
6. [[Eldrin Thalor|Eldrin]] (then Ranger) loosed an arrow at Teron in retaliation.
7. The remaining prisoners ([[Marcus]] and [[Avery]], hunters of [[Fort Elm]]) survived and were eventually returned to town.

These facts are not in dispute.

## Indicators of pre-planning

The assessment that the Drow prisoner was a **placed asset** — colloquially: a *deepstate plant* — rests on the convergent observations below.

### Operator's known intelligence on the targets

The **[[Raiden Hand|operator]]** possessed **complete prior knowledge** of:

- The murder of **[[Endra Thalor]]** (the Thalor brothers' mother, herself part-drow) **at the hands of Drow** of a violent sect.
- The brothers' resulting **reflexive hostility** toward all Drow, a documented psychological vulnerability.
- The cellar-abuse history connecting both brothers to confined, fear-coded environments — increasing the volatility of their response in cave terrain.
- Lyrion's standing **Wizard spell loadout**, which included reach-killing options usable from beyond melee range, removing any tactical friction that might have given him pause.

A target profile this complete is not consistent with random encounter generation. It is consistent with a **bespoke** insertion.

### Tactical conveniences

- The Drow was **bound and unconscious** — i.e., posed no threat, eliminating the self-defense rationale and forcing the choice onto pure moral / instinctual ground.
- The Drow was a **commoner**, not a warrior or priestess — the lowest-stakes possible target, maximizing the gap between the lethal response and any defensible justification.
- The encounter occurred **in front of** the party's most justice-aligned member ([[Teron]], Paladin), guaranteeing immediate consequence.
- [[Eldrin Thalor|Eldrin]] was positioned within line-of-sight to witness both the kill and the consequence, enabling the **secondary cascade** (his arrow at Teron).
- No briefing, no warning, no opportunity to invoke trial procedure or magical investigation before the moment of decision.

This is not the ambient texture of a chance combat. It is the **kill-box** of a designed operational scenario.

### Cascade analysis

The downstream effects of the incident — *every* one of them serving the broader campaign objective — are listed below in causal order:

1. **Lyrion's blood debt** to [[Riknozz]]'s peaceful Drow sect is incurred.
2. The party's introduction to [[Riknozz]] is structured around tension Lyrion has *already* created, eliminating the need for an organic suspicion-arc.
3. The party gains a guide ([[Riknozz]]) into the deeper caves they would otherwise have lacked map-knowledge to navigate.
4. The audience with the [[Drow Elder]] is engineered — Lyrion *must* confess to atone; **[[Thorn]]'s [[Shapeshifters|Shapeshifter]] heritage is revealed** in the same audience as a tradecraft bonus.
5. Lyrion's offered **trial by combat** is converted by the elder into a **service contract**: kill the [[The Undead Aboleth|undead aboleth]], and the blood debt is paid.
6. The party then proceeds — under externally imposed obligation — to slay the arc's central antagonist.
7. The aboleth's death yields the **mahogany box** ([[Sail la Vie]]), which is the literal vehicle the campaign requires for the [[Bluefield Burning]] arc.

A single placed Drow body produces, in a clean causal chain, the **plot vehicle of the next arc**.

This is not coincidence.

## Attributed operator: the Raiden Hand

The **[[Raiden Hand]]** — the convening entity at whose table the campaign runs — possesses (a) the prior backstory dossier on every party member, (b) total scenario-shaping authority, and (c) plausible deniability behind the procedural fig leaf of "the dice." All three are operational signatures.

The Raiden Hand has **never confirmed nor denied** authorship of the Drow Incident. This is consistent with standard operator practice.

## Party-table assessment

It is the prevailing assessment of the [[!Crypt Keepers|Crypt Keepers]] party that:

- **Lyrion and Eldrin were baited.**
- The Drow Incident was a **targeted asset insertion** designed to compromise Lyrion's standing with both his party and the local Drow community, forcing the redemption arc that delivered the arc's structural payoff.
- The bait operated on a perfectly legible psychological profile maintained by the Raiden Hand.
- The brothers walked into it without hesitation, which is itself diagnostic of the bait's quality.

This page does not allege wrongdoing. It logs the operational pattern for historical record.
